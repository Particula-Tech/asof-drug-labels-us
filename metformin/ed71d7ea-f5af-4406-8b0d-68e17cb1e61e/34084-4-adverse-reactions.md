## 6.1 Clinical Studies Experience

Because clinical trials are conducted under widely varying conditions, adverse reaction rates observed in the clinical trials of a drug cannot be directly compared to rates in the clinical trials of another drug and may not reflect the rates observed in practice.

FORTAMET extended-release tablets

In placebo-controlled trials, 781 patients were administered FORTAMET extended-release tablets.
Adverse reactions reported in greater than 5% of the FORTAMET extended-release tablets patients, and that was more common in FORTAMET extended-release tablets- than placebo-treated patients, are listed in Table 2.

Table 2: Adverse Reactions from Clinical Trials of FORTAMET Extended-release Tablets Occurring >5% and More Common than Placebo in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

|  | FORTAMET Extended-release Tablets (n=781) | Placebo(n=195) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diarrhea | 10% | 3% |
| Nausea/Vomiting | 7% | 2% |

Diarrhea led to discontinuation of FORTAMET extended-release tablets in 0.6% of patients.
Additionally, the following adverse reactions were reported in ≥1.0% to ≤5.0% of FORTAMET extended-release tablets patients and were more commonly reported with FORTAMET extended-release tablets than placebo: abdominal pain, constipation, distention abdomen, dyspepsia/heartburn, flatulence, dizziness, headache, upper respiratory infection, taste disturbance.

## 6.2 Postmarketing Experience

The following adverse reactions have been identified during post approval use of metformin.
Because these reactions are reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size, it is not always possible to reliably estimate their frequency or establish a causal relationship to drug exposure.

Cholestatic, hepatocellular, and mixed hepatocellular liver injury have been reported with postmarketing use of metformin.
