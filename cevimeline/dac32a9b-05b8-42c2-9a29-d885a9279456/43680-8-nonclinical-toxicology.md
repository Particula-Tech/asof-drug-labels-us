# 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY

## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility

Carcinogenesis

Oral carcinogenicity studies were conducted in mice and rats.
An increase in the incidence of adenocarcinomas of the uterus was observed in female rats that received cevimeline 100 mg/kg/day (8 times the MRHD based on AUC comparison).
No drug-related tumors were observed in male rats at oral doses up to 100 mg/kg/day (5 times the MRHD based on AUC comparison) or in female rats at oral doses up to 50 mg/kg/day (4 times the MRHD based on AUC comparison).
No drug-related tumors were observed in mice at oral doses up to 300 mg/kg/day (12 times the MRHD based on AUC comparison).

Mutagenesis

Cevimeline exhibited no evidence of mutagenicity or clastogenicity in a battery of assays that included the Ames assay, an in vitrochromosomal aberration assay in mammalian cells, a mouse lymphoma assay in L5178Y cells, or a micronucleus assay conducted in mice.

Impairment of Fertility

Cevimeline did not adversely affect the reproductive performance or fertility of male rats when administered for 63 days prior to mating and throughout the period of mating at oral doses up to 45 mg/kg/day (5 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).

Female rats that were treated with cevimeline at an oral dose of 45 mg/kg/day (5 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison) from 14 days prior to mating through gestation day 7 exhibited decreased implantation.
No adverse effects were noted at oral doses up to 3 mg/kg/day (0.3 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).
