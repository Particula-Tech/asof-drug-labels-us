# 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS

## 8.1 Pregnancy

Risk Summary

There are no available data on EVOXAC use in pregnant women to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes.
In animal reproduction studies, oral administration of cevimeline to pregnant rats and rabbits during the period of organogenesis did not cause significant embryofetal toxicity at doses up to 6 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) in rats or 10 times the MRHD in rabbits, at which maternal toxicities were noted (see Data).

The background risk of major birth defects and miscarriage for the indicated population are unknown.
All pregnancies have a background risk of birth defect, loss, or other adverse outcomes.
In the U.S. general population, the estimated background risk of major birth defects and miscarriage in clinically recognized pregnancies is 2% to 4% and 15% to 20%, respectively.

Data

Animal Data

In an embryofetal development study, cevimeline was administered to pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis at oral doses of 5, 30, and 60 mg/kg/day.
Cholinergic stimulation (e.g., mydriasis, salivation, lacrimation, diarrhea) was noted at doses ≥30 mg/kg/day (≥3 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).
Maternal mortality was observed at 60 mg/kg/day (6 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).
No significant embryofetal toxicity was observed at doses up to 60 mg/kg/day.
In another embryofetal development study, cevimeline was administered to pregnant rabbits during the period of organogenesis at oral doses of 0.2, 3, and 45 mg/kg/day.
Increased salivation and tachypnea were noted at 45 mg/kg/day (10 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).
No significant embryofetal toxicity was noted at doses up to 45 mg/kg/day.

In a pre- and postnatal development study in rats, cevimeline was administered to pregnant rats during pregnancy and lactation periods at oral doses of 3, 12, and 45 mg/kg/day.
Maternal toxicities (decreased body weight and mortality) were noted at 45 mg/kg/day (5 times the MRHD based on mg/m 2dose comparison).
No significant developmental toxicity was noted at doses up to 45 mg/kg/day.

## 8.2 Lactation

Risk Summary

There is no data on the presence of cevimeline or its metabolite in human or animal milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production.
Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed infant, advise patients that breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment with EVOXAC and for one day after the last dose (approximately 5 elimination half-lives) [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)].

## 8.4 Pediatric Use

Safety and effectiveness of EVOXAC have not been established in pediatric patients.

## 8.5 Geriatric Use

Clinical trials of EVOXAC did not include sufficient numbers of subjects 65 years of age and older to determine whether they respond differently from younger adult subjects.

## 8.6 CYP2D6 Poor Metabolizers

Closely monitor patients with known or suspected CYP2D6 deficiency.
Patients known or suspected to be deficient in CYP2D6 activity may be at a higher risk for EVOXAC-associated adverse reactions [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)].
