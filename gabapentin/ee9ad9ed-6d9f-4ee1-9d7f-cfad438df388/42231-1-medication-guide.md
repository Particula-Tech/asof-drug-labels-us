# MEDICATION GUIDE

NEURONTIN (Neu rŏn' tĭn) (Gabapentin)

Capsules, Tablets, and Oral Solution

Read the Medication Guide before you or your family member start taking NEURONTIN and each time you get a refill.
There may be new information.
This information does not take the place of talking to your healthcare provider about your medical condition or treatment.
If you have any questions about NEURONTIN, ask your healthcare provider or pharmacist.

What is the most important information I should know about NEURONTIN?

- 1.Like other antiepileptic drugs, NEURONTIN may cause suicidal thoughts or actions in a very small number of people, about 1 in 500.
  Call a healthcare provider right away if you have any of these symptoms, especially if they are new, worse, or worry you:
  - thoughts about suicide or dying
  - attempts to commit suicide
  - new or worse depression
  - new or worse anxiety
  - feeling agitated or restless
  - panic attacks
  - trouble sleeping (insomnia)
  - new or worse irritability
  - acting aggressive, being angry, or violent
  - acting on dangerous impulses
  - an extreme increase in activity and talking (mania)
  - other unusual changes in behavior or mood

If you have suicidal thoughts or actions, do not stop NEURONTIN without first talking to your healthcare provider.

- Stopping NEURONTIN suddenly can increase the possibility of side effects including increased seizure frequency.
- Suicidal thoughts or actions can be caused by things other than medicines.
  If you have suicidal thoughts or actions, your healthcare provider may check for other causes.

How can I watch for early symptoms of suicidal thoughts and actions?

- Pay attention to any changes, especially sudden changes, in mood, behaviors, thoughts, or feelings.
- Keep all follow-up visits with your healthcare provider as scheduled.
- Call your healthcare provider between visits as needed, especially if you are worried about symptoms.

What else do I need to know about NEURONTIN?

- NEURONTIN can cause dizziness, sleepiness, or other symptoms and signs of depression.
  You should not drive a car or operate other complex machinery until you know how you will react to NEURONTIN.
- If you require treatment with morphine at the same time as you are taking NEURONTIN, talk to your healthcare provider, as your dose of NEURONTIN and/or morphine may need to be adjusted.
- If you take an antacid such as Maalox®, you should wait at least 2 hours before taking your next dose of NEURONTIN.
- If you become pregnant, you should talk to your healthcare provider immediately.
  You should also consider enrolling in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry.
  This registry is collecting information about the safety of the use of all antiepileptic drugs during pregnancy.
  To enroll, call the toll free number 1-888-233-2334.
- NEURONTIN is secreted into human breast milk.
  The effect of NEURONTIN on the nursing infant is unknown; therefore, you should talk to your healthcare provider before nursing your infant while on NEURONTIN.
- If you have epilepsy, tell your healthcare provider immediately if your seizures get worse or if you have any new types of seizures.

What is NEURONTIN?

NEURONTIN is a prescription medicine used for treatment of:

- Pain from damaged nerves (postherpetic pain) that follows healing of shingles (a painful rash that comes after a herpes zoster infection) in adults.
- Partial seizures when taken together with other medications in patients over 12 years of age with epilepsy and in pediatric patients aged 3 to 12 years.

Who should not take NEURONTIN?

Do not take NEURONTIN if you are allergic to any of its ingredients.
The active ingredient is gabapentin.
See the section "What are the ingredients in NEURONTIN?" for a complete list of ingredients.

NEURONTIN has not been studied for the treatment of postherpetic pain in pediatric patients and so should not be taken for treatment of this condition in children.

NEURONTIN should not be given for the treatment of partial seizures in pediatric patients younger than age 3 years.

What should I tell my healthcare provider before taking NEURONTIN?

Before starting NEURONTIN, tell your healthcare provider about all medications you take (including prescription and nonprescription medications, vitamins, and herbal medicines, as well as alcohol consumption).
Tell your healthcare provider about all of your medical conditions, including if you:

- Have or have had kidney problems or are on hemodialysis
- Are pregnant or plan to become pregnant.
  NEURONTIN should not be taken during pregnancy, unless you are told otherwise by your healthcare provider.
  If you discover you are pregnant, do not suddenly discontinue taking NEURONTIN as this may lead to serious consequences for you and your baby.
- Are breast feeding.
  Taking NEURONTIN while breast feeding it is not recommended unless your healthcare provider tells you to do so.
- Are taking other medications, including prescription and nonprescription medicines, vitamins, herbal supplements, and alcohol.
  Some medications can affect the way NEURONTIN works or NEURONTIN itself can affect the way other medications work.
  These include any medications containing morphine and antacids.

How should I take NEURONTIN?

- Take NEURONTIN exactly as prescribed.
  Your healthcare provider may need to change (adjust) the dose of NEURONTIN until it is right for you.
  If you miss a dose of NEURONTIN, take the missed dose as soon as you remember.
  If it is almost time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and take your next dose at the regular time.
  If taking capsules or tablets, always swallow them whole with plenty of water.
- NEURONTIN can be taken with or without food.
- Do not stop taking NEURONTIN unless your healthcare provider tells you to do so.
  If your treatment is stopped, it should be done gradually over a minimum of 1 week.
  If you stop taking NEURONTIN suddenly or before your healthcare provider tells you, there is an increased risk of seizures, pain, or discomfort.
  Adverse events following abrupt discontinuation include the following: anxiety, difficulty sleeping, feeling sick, pain, sweating and chest pain.
  Please tell your healthcare provider if you have any of these symptoms.
- If you do not think that you are getting better or if you think that NEURONTIN is either too strong or too weak, talk to your healthcare provider or pharmacist as soon as possible.

What should I avoid while taking NEURONTIN?

See the section above "Who should not take NEURONTIN".

Do not drive a car or operate complex, hazardous machinery until you know how NEURONTIN affects you.

What are the possible side effects of NEURONTIN?

Like all medicines, NEURONTIN can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.
Tell your healthcare provider immediately if you experience any of the following symptoms after taking NEURONTIN.
Although they are very rare, these symptoms can be serious.

- swelling of the lips and face, skin rash and redness and/or hair loss as these may be symptoms of a serious allergic reaction
- If you suffer from kidney problems or are on hemodialysis, tell your healthcare provider if you develop muscle pain and/or weakness
- persistent stomach pain, feeling sick and being sick as these may be symptoms of acute pancreatitis

The most common side effects of Neurontin are:

- dizziness
- lack of coordination
- viral infection
- feeling drowsy
- feeling tired
- fever
- convulsions
- jerky movements
- difficulty with speaking
- loss of memory
- tremor
- difficulty sleeping
- headache
- sensitive skin
- decreased sensation
- difficulty with coordination
- unusual eye movement
- increased, decreased or absent reflexes

Tell your healthcare provider about any side effect that bothers you or that does not go away.

Additionally, in clinical studies in children, aggressive behavior and jerky movements were reported.

Adverse events following the abrupt discontinuation of NEURONTIN include: anxiety, difficulty sleeping, feeling sick, pain, sweating, and chest pain.

These are not all the possible side effects of NEURONTIN.
For more information, ask your healthcare provider or pharmacist.

Call your doctor for medical advice about side effects.
You may report side effects to FDA at 1-800-FDA-1088.

How should I store NEURONTIN?

Keep NEURONTIN out of the reach of children.

Store NEURONTIN Capsules at 77°F (25°C) or between 59°F – 86°F (15°C – 30°C).

Store NEURONTIN Tablets at 77°F (25°C) or between 59°F – 86°F (15°C – 30°C).

Refrigerate NEURONTIN Oral Solution between 36°F – 46°F (2°C – 8°C).

General information about NEURONTIN

Medicines are sometimes prescribed for purposes other than those listed in a Medication Guide.
Do not use NEURONTIN for a condition for which it was not prescribed.
Do not give NEURONTIN to other people, even if they have the same condition.
It may harm them.

This Medication Guide summarizes the most important information about NEURONTIN.
If you would like more information, talk with your healthcare provider.
You can ask your healthcare provider or pharmacist for information about NEURONTIN that was written for healthcare professionals.

What are the ingredients in NEURONTIN?

Active ingredient: gabapentin

Inactive ingredients in the capsules: lactose, cornstarch, and talc.
The 100-mg capsule shell contains gelatin and titanium dioxide.
The 300-mg capsule shell contains gelatin, titanium dioxide, and iron oxide.
The 400-mg capsule shell contains gelatin, red iron oxide, titanium dioxide, and iron oxide.
The imprinting ink contains FD&C Blue No. 2 and titanium dioxide.

Inactive ingredients in the tablets: poloxamer 407, copolyvidonum, cornstarch, magnesium stearate, hydroxypropyl cellulose, talc, candelilla wax, and purified water.

Inactive ingredients in the oral solution: glycerin, xylitol, purified water, and artificial flavor.
