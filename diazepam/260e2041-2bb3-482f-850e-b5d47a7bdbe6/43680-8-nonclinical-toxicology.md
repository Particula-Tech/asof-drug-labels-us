# 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY

## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility

Carcinogenesis

An increased incidence of liver tumors was observed in male mice and rats that were administered diazepam in the diet for 80 and 104 weeks, respectively at a dose of 75 mg/kg/day, approximately 9 and 18 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on mg/m2 body surface area, respectively.

Mutagenesis

The data currently available are inadequate to determine the mutagenic potential of diazepam.

Impairment of Fertility

Reproduction studies in rats showed decreases in the number of pregnancies and in the number of surviving offspring following administration of an oral dose of 100 mg/kg/day (approximately 24 times the MRHD based on mg/m2 body surface area) prior to and during mating and throughout gestation and lactation.
No adverse effects on fertility or offspring viability were noted up to a dose of 80 mg/kg/day (approximately 19 times the MRHD based on mg/m2 body surface area).
