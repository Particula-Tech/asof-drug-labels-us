# 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY

## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility

Carcinogenesis Chronic dietary administration of clonidine was not carcinogenic to rats (132 weeks) or mice (78 weeks) dosed, respectively, at up to 10 or 15 times the maximum recommended daily human dose as mg/kg (2 or 1 times the MRDHD on a mg/m2 basis).
Mutagenicity There was no evidence of genotoxicity in the Ames test for mutagenicity or mouse micronucleus test for clastogenicity.
Impairment of Fertility In a reproduction study fertility of female rats appeared to be adversely affected at dose levels of 0.5 and 2.0 mg/kg/day (2 and 8 times the MRHD on a mg/m2 basis).
Lower doses have not been adequately evaluated and a no adverse effect level could not be established.
