# 14 CLINICAL STUDIES

## 14.1 Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

The clinical efficacy of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE has been evaluated in two (ETHOS and KRONOS) randomized, double-blind, multicenter, parallel-group trials in subjects with moderate to very severe COPD who remained symptomatic while receiving 2 or more inhaled maintenance treatments for COPD for at least 6 weeks prior to screening.

ETHOS (NCT02465567) was conducted over 52 weeks in a total of 8,588 subjects randomized (1:1:1:1) to receive BREZTRI AEROSPHERE (budesonide/glycopyrrolate/formoterol fumarate 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg), budesonide, glycopyrrolate and formoterol fumarate (BGF MDI 160 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg) (the BGF MDI 160 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg dosing regimen is not approved), glycopyrrolate and formoterol fumarate (GFF MDI 18 mcg/9.6 mcg), or budesonide and formoterol fumarate (BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg), all administered twice daily.
GFF MDI and BFF MDI used the same inhaler and excipients as BREZTRI AEROSPHERE.

ETHOS was conducted in subjects with a history of 1 or more moderate or severe exacerbations in the year prior to screening, post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio less than 0.7 and the post-bronchodilator FEV1 less than 65% predicted normal value.

The population demographics across all treatments in ETHOS were: mean age of 65 years, 60% male, 85% Caucasian, and an average smoking history of 48 pack-years, with 41% identified as current smokers.
The mean post-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV1 was 43% (range 16% to 73%).
At study entry, the most common COPD medications were ICS + long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) + LABA (39%), ICS + LABA (31%), and LAMA + LABA (14%).

In ETHOS, the primary endpoint was the rate of moderate or severe COPD exacerbations for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE compared with GFF MDI and BFF MDI.

KRONOS (NCT02497001) was conducted over 24 weeks, in a total of 1,896 subjects randomized (2:2:1:1) to receive BREZTRI AEROSPHERE (budesonide/glycopyrrolate/formoterol fumarate 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg), glycopyrrolate and formoterol fumarate (GFF MDI 18 mcg/9.6 mcg), budesonide and formoterol fumarate (BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg), or open-label active comparator, all administered twice daily.
GFF MDI and BFF MDI used the same inhaler and excipients as BREZTRI AEROSPHERE.

KRONOS was conducted in subjects with a screening post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio less than 0.7 and post-bronchodilator FEV1 less than 80% predicted normal value.
Subjects in KRONOS were not required to have a history of moderate or severe exacerbations in the year prior to screening.

The population demographics across all treatments in KRONOS were: mean age of 65 years, 71% male, 50% Caucasian, 45% Asian, and an average smoking history of 52 pack-years, with 40% identified as current smokers.
The mean post-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV1 was 50% (range 22% to 84%).
At study entry, the most common COPD medications were ICS + LAMA + LABA (27%), ICS + LABA (38%), and LAMA + LABA (20%).

In KRONOS, the primary endpoints were FEV1 area under the curve from 0-4 hours (FEV1 AUC0-4) at Week 24 for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE compared to BFF MDI and change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 at Week 24 for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE compared to GFF MDI.

Lung Function

In ETHOS, a subset of subjects were included in a spirometric sub-study with primary endpoints of FEV1 AUC0-4 at Week 24 (mL) and change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 at Week 24 (mL).
BREZTRI AEROSPHERE demonstrated an increase in on-treatment FEV1 AUC0-4 and trough FEV1 at Week 24 relative to BFF MDI and GFF MDI (Table 2).
The effects on lung function (mean change from baseline in on-treatment morning pre-dose trough FEV1) of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE compared with GFF MDI and BFF MDI were observed at all timepoints over the course of the study (Figure 1).

In KRONOS, BREZTRI AEROSPHERE demonstrated an increase in on-treatment FEV1 AUC0-4 at Week 24 relative to BFF MDI and an increase in mean change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 at Week 24 compared with GFF MDI (Table 4).
The comparison of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE with GFF MDI in mean change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 at Week 24 was not statistically significant.

In both trials, there were consistent improvements in lung function in patient subgroups classified by age, sex, degree of airflow limitation (moderate, severe, and very severe), and previous ICS use.

In KRONOS, the median time to onset on Day 1, defined as a 100 mL increase from baseline in FEV1, was within 5 minutes in subjects receiving BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg.
In ETHOS, subjects treated with BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg on average used less daily rescue medication over 24 weeks compared to subjects treated with GFF MDI 18 mcg/9.6 mcg and BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg.

Exacerbations

In ETHOS, the primary endpoint was the rate of on-treatment moderate or severe COPD exacerbations in subjects treated with BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg compared with GFF MDI and BFF MDI.

Exacerbations were defined as worsening of 2 or more major symptoms (dyspnea, sputum volume, and sputum color) or worsening of any 1 major symptom together with any 1 of the following minor symptoms: cough, wheeze, sore throat, colds (nasal discharge and/or nasal congestion), and fever without other cause for at least 2 consecutive days.
Exacerbations were considered to be moderate severity if treatment with systemic corticosteroids and/or antibiotics was required and were considered to be severe if they resulted in hospitalization or death.

In ETHOS, treatment with BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg demonstrated a reduction in the rate of on-treatment moderate or severe COPD exacerbations over 52 weeks compared with GFF MDI and BFF MDI (see Table 5).

Health-Related Quality of Life

In both trials, health-related quality of life was assessed using the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) responder analysis which was defined as an improvement in SGRQ score from baseline of 4 or more.

In ETHOS, the on-treatment percentage of SGRQ responders at Week 24 was greater for subjects treated with BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg (50%) compared with both GFF MDI 18 mcg/9.6 mcg (43%; odds ratio 1.4; 95% CI: 1.2, 1.5) and BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg (45%; odds ratio 1.2; 95% CI: 1.1, 1.4).
Similar differences between treatments were observed at Week 52.

In KRONOS, the on-treatment percentage of SGRQ responders at Week 24 was greater for subjects treated with BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg (50%) compared with both GFF MDI 18 mcg/9.6 mcg (44%; odds ratio 1.3; 95% CI: 1.0, 1.6) and BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg (43%; odds ratio 1.3; 95% CI: 1.0, 1.7).

## 14.2 Asthma

The efficacy of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE for the maintenance treatment of asthma was evaluated in two randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, active-controlled, multicenter clinical trials (KALOS [NCT04609878] and LOGOS [NCT04609904]) of 24 to 52-weeks duration.
The trials enrolled adult and pediatric subjects 12 years of age and older with asthma who were inadequately controlled, defined by an Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ)-7 score ≥1.5 despite treatment with a medium or high dose of inhaled corticosteroid/long-acting beta2-agonist (ICS/LABA), and pre-bronchodilator FEV1 <80% predicted normal value for participants ≥18 years of age or <90% predicted normal value for participants 12 to <18 years of age.
Subjects who met initial eligibility criteria entered a 5-week run-in period of treatment with BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg twice daily.
Subjects who remained inadequately controlled (ACQ-7 ≥1.5) were randomized (1:1:1:1) to receive a dose of BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg, BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/18 mcg/9.6 mcg, BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg, or budesonide and formoterol fumarate (BUD/FORM MDI) 320 mcg/9 mcg, all administered as two inhalations twice daily.
BFF MDI used the same inhaler and aerosphere delivery platform as BREZTRI AEROSPHERE.
The efficacy population is based on 1200 subjects in the KALOS trial and 1187 subjects in the LOGOS trial who received BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg or BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg twice daily by oral inhalation.

The demographics of the efficacy population in KALOS were: mean age of 52 years (range: 12 to 80 years), 63% female, 65% White, 24% Asian, 2% Black or African American, <1% American Indian or Alaska Native, 8% other; and 30% of Hispanic or Latino ethnicity.
Subjects had a median asthma duration of 16 years (range: 1.1-74 years) and 65% of subjects reported having at least one severe exacerbation in the prior year.
At baseline, the mean pre-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV1 was 59% (standard deviation [SD] 12%), the mean reversibility was 23% (SD 19%), and the mean ACQ-5 score was 2.5 (SD 0.7).

The demographics of the efficacy population in LOGOS were: mean age of 52 years (range: 12 to 80 years), 61% female, 45% White, 38% Asian, 6% Black or African American, 1% American Indian or Alaska Native, 10% other; and 12% of Hispanic or Latino ethnicity.
Subjects had a median asthma duration of 15 years (range: 1.1-76 years) and 48% of subjects reported having at least 1 severe exacerbation in the prior year.
At baseline, the mean pre-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV1 was 59% (SD 13%), the mean reversibility was 22% (SD 18%), and the mean ACQ-5 score was 2.5 (SD 0.7).

Lung Function

The primary endpoint in KALOS and LOGOS was a change from baseline in FEV1 area under the curve from 0-3 hours (FEV1 AUC0-3) post-dose at Week 24 for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg compared to BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg.

The key secondary endpoint in KALOS and LOGOS was a change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 at Week 24 for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg compared to BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg.

In both KALOS and LOGOS, subjects receiving BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg resulted in statistically significant increases in change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 and change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 compared to BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg at Week 24 (Table 6).

Severe Exacerbations

The rate of severe asthma exacerbations endpoint was based on the pooled KALOS and LOGOS trials.
A severe asthma exacerbation was defined as a deterioration of asthma symptoms that required at least 3 days of systemic corticosteroids or an emergency department or urgent care visit for asthma symptoms requiring treatment with systemic corticosteroids or resulted in in-patient hospitalization, and/or death.
Asthma exacerbations were assessed over the blinded treatment period.

In the pooled efficacy analysis (KALOS and LOGOS), the adjusted mean annualized rate of severe asthma exacerbations was 0.54 for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg (398 of 1179 patients, 34%) and 0.60 for BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg (444 of 1208 patients, 37%).

The rate ratio of severe asthma exacerbations for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg compared to BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg was 0.90 (95% CI 0.78, 1.03).

Asthma Control and Health-Related Quality of Life

Asthma control was assessed at Week 24 in each trial using the Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ).
The ACQ-7 incorporates 5 questions on symptoms, plus FEV1, and rescue bronchodilator use.
ACQ-7 responder was defined as a decrease in score of ≥0.5.

The ACQ-7 responder rate was 65% for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg and 64% for BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg at Week 24 (OR 1.05; 95% CI 0.82, 1.34) in KALOS.
The ACQ-7 responder rate was 68% for BREZTRI AEROSPHERE 320 mcg/36 mcg/9.6 mcg and 64% for BFF MDI 320 mcg/9.6 mcg at Week 24 (OR 1.27; 95% CI 0.98, 1.63) in LOGOS.

Responder rates with ACQ-5 (comprising the 5 questions on symptoms from ACQ-7) were similar to those with ACQ-7 at Week 24.
