# ANIMAL PHARMACOLOGY

Biochemical studies in animals have suggested reasons for the prolonged effect of chlorthalidone.
Absorption from the gastrointestinal tract is slow due to its low solubility.
After passage to the liver, some of the drug enters the general circulation, while some is excreted in the bile, to be reabsorbed later.
In the general circulation, it is distributed widely to the tissue, but is taken up in highest concentrations by the kidneys, where amounts have been found 72 hours after ingestion, long after it has disappeared from other tissues.
The drug is excreted unchanged in the urine.
