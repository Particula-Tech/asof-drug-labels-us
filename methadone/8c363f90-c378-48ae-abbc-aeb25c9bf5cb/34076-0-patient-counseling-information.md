# 17 PATIENT COUNSELING INFORMATION

1. Advise the patient to read the FDA-approved patient labeling (Medication Guide).

Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse

1. Inform patients that the use of methadone, even when taken as recommended, can result in addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose or death [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1)].
   Instruct patients not to share methadone with others and to take steps to protect methadone from theft or misuse.
2. 

Life-threatening Respiratory Depression

1. Inform patients of the risk of life-threatening respiratory depression, including information that the risk is greatest when starting methadone or when the dose is increased, and that it can occur even at recommended doses [see Warnings and Precautions (5.2)].
   Advise patients how to recognize respiratory depression and to seek medical attention if breathing difficulties develop.
2. 

Accidental Ingestion

1. Inform patients that accidental ingestion, especially in children, may result in respiratory depression or death [see Warnings and Precautions (5.2)].
   Instruct patients to take steps to store methadone securely and to dispose of unused methadone by flushing the tablets down the toilet.
2. 

Symptoms of Arrhythmia

1. Instruct patients to seek medical attention immediately if they experience symptoms suggestive of an arrhythmia (such as palpitations, near syncope, or syncope) when taking methadone.
2. 

Interactions with Benzodiazepines and Other CNS Depressants

1. Inform patients and caregivers that potentially fatal additive effects may occur if Methadone Hydrochloride Oral Solution is used with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants, including alcohol, and not to use these concomitantly unless supervised by a health care provider [see Warnings and Precautions (5.5), Drug Interactions (7.1)].
2. 

Serotonin Syndrome

1. Inform patients that Methadone Hydrochloride Oral Solution could cause a rare but potentially life-threatening condition resulting from concomitant administration of serotonergic drugs.
   Warn patients of the symptoms of serotonin syndrome and to seek medical attention right away if symptoms develop.
   Instruct patients to inform their physicians if they are taking, or plan to take serotonergic medications [see Warnings and Precautions (5.8), Drug Interactions (7.5)].
2. 

Adrenal Insufficiency

1. Inform patients that Methadone Hydrochloride Oral Solution could cause adrenal insufficiency, a potentially lifethreatening condition.
   Adrenal insufficiency may present with non-specific symptoms and signs such as nausea, vomiting, anorexia, fatigue, weakness, dizziness, and low blood pressure.
   Advise patients to seek medical attention if they experience a constellation of these symptoms [see Warnings and Precautions (5.9)].
2. 

Important Administration Instructions

1. Instruct patients how to properly take methadone, including the following:
2. •Use methadone exactly as prescribed to reduce the risk of life-threatening adverse reactions (e.g., respiratory depression)
3. •Do not discontinue methadone without first discussing the need for a tapering regimen with the prescriber

Hypotension

1. Inform patients that methadone may cause orthostatic hypotension and syncope.
   Instruct patients how to recognize symptoms of low blood pressure and how to reduce the risk of serious consequences should hypotension occur (e.g., sit or lie down, carefully rise from a sitting or lying position).

Driving or Operating Heavy Machinery

1. Inform patients that methadone may impair the ability to perform potentially hazardous activities such as driving a car or operating heavy machinery.
   Advise patients not to perform such tasks until they know how they will react to the medication.
2. 

Constipation

1. Advise patients of the potential for severe constipation, including management instructions and when to seek medical attention.
2. 

Anaphylaxis

1. Inform patients that anaphylaxis has been reported with ingredients contained in methadone.
   Advise patients how to recognize such a reaction and when to seek medical attention.
2. 

Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome

1. Advise women that if they are pregnant while being treated with Methadone Hydrochloride Oral Solution, the baby may have signs of withdrawal at birth and that withdrawal is treatable [see Warnings and Precautions (5.4), Specific Populations (8.1)].
2. 

Breastfeeding

1. Instruct nursing mothers using methadone to watch for signs of methadone toxicity in their infants, which include increased sleepiness (more than usual), difficulty breastfeeding, breathing difficulties, or limpness.
   Instruct nursing mothers to talk to the baby’s healthcare provider immediately if they notice these signs.
   If they cannot reach the healthcare provider right away, instruct them to take the baby to the emergency room or call 911 (or local emergency services).
2. 

Disposal of Unused Methadone

1. Advise patients to flush the unused methadone down the toilet when methadone is no longer needed.
