# 14 CLINICAL STUDIES

Methylphenidate HCl Extended-Release Capsules CD was evaluated in a double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled 3-week trial (Study 1) in which 321 untreated or previously treated pediatric patients 6 to 15 years of age with a DSM-IV diagnosis of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).
Patients were required to have the combined or predominantly hyperactive-impulsive subtype of ADHD; patients with the predominantly inattentive subtype were excluded.
Patients randomized to the Methylphenidate HCl Extended-Release Capsules CD group received 20 mg daily in the morning for the first week.
Their Methylphenidate HCl Extended-Release Capsules CD dosage could be increased weekly to a maximum of 60 mg by the third week, depending on individual response to treatment.

The patient’s regular school teacher completed the teachers’ version of the Conners’ Global Index Scale (TCGIS), a scale for assessing ADHD symptoms, in the morning and again in the afternoon on three alternate days of each treatment week.
The primary efficacy endpoint was determined by the average of the total scores for the 10-item TCGIS completed by the classroom teacher in the morning and again in the afternoon on the three observation days during the last week of double-blind therapy.
Patients in the Methylphenidate HCl Extended-Release Capsules CD group showed a statistically significant improvement in symptom scores from baseline over patients in the placebo group (See Figure 2).
Separate analyses of TCGIS scores in the morning and afternoon revealed superiority in improvement in the Methylphenidate HCl Extended-Release Capsules CD group over the placebo group during both time periods (See Figure 3).
